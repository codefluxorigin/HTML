Mostly asked questions.

1. What is a court marriage?

A court marriage is a legal union solemnized by a Marriage Officer under the Special Marriage Act, 1954. It is conducted directly through the government without any religious rituals or ceremonies, and it is open to all Indian citizens regardless of their religion, caste, or creed.

2. What are the basic eligibility requirements for a court marriage?

To be eligible for a court marriage in India, the parties must fulfill the following conditions at the time of marriage:

Age: The bride must be at least 18 years old, and the groom must be at least 21 years old.

Mental Capacity: Both parties must be capable of giving valid consent and must not suffer from any mental disorder that makes them unfit for marriage.

Monogamy: Neither party should have a living spouse at the time of marriage (both must be unmarried, divorced, or widowed).

Prohibited Degrees: The parties must not fall within the degrees of prohibited relationships specified by their respective personal laws (unless permitted by their custom)

3. What documents are required for a court marriage?

Generally, both parties need to submit the following documents along with the application form: Completed application form with the prescribed fee. Passport-size photographs of both parties. Proof of Age: Birth certificate, Matriculation/School leaving certificate, or Passport. Proof of Address/Residence: Aadhaar card, Voter ID, Passport, or Utility bill. Affidavit: Stating marital status (unmarried, divorced, or widowed), date of birth, and that the parties are not within prohibited relationship degrees. If previously married: Divorce decree (for divorcees) or death certificate of the former spouse (for widows/widowers). Witness Documents: PAN card, address proof, and photographs of three witnesses.

4. What is the procedure for a court marriage?

Notice of Intended Marriage: The couple must submit a notice in the prescribed format to the Marriage Officer of the district where at least one of the parties has resided for not less than 30 days preceding the date of notice. Publication of Notice: The Marriage Officer publishes the notice by pasting it in a conspicuous place in their office to invite any objections. Waiting Period (30 Days): Any person can object to the marriage within 30 days from the date of publication on legal grounds. Solemnization: If no objections are raised (or if objections are legally cleared), the couple—along with three witnesses—appears before the Marriage Officer on the appointed date to sign the marriage certificate.

5. Who can act as a witness for a court marriage?

Any responsible person who can properly identify the bride and groom can act as a witness. Typically, three witnesses are required. They can be friends, relatives, or colleagues, and they must carry valid identity and address proof (like an Aadhaar card or PAN card).

6. How long does the entire court marriage process take?

The minimum time required is 30 days (due to the mandatory 30-day notice period for public objections). In practice, depending on the district marriage office's schedule and paperwork verification, the total process usually takes anywhere from 30 to 45 days.
